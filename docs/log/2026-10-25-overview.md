# 2026-10-25 — The overview: numbers at a glance, and a page per item

The reader liked the timeline but wanted a separate view with the main figures of the
sites, servers and services — widgets that give the whole picture at a glance — and a full
page behind each one. Decision: [0046](../decisions/0046-an-overview-of-items-rendered-by-the-hub.md).

## How it is drawn

Four options were weighed: a skin the hub renders; the same with a TypeScript client and a
charting library; Grafana over `/api/v1/history`; numbers added to the timeline's lanes. The
hub renders it: everything a client would bring — zone, catalogue, refresh — is already
server-side ([0035](../decisions/0035-mission-control-is-rendered-by-the-hub.md)), and an
interactive chart can later replace one consumer of the history endpoint.

## Five questions, one at a time

1. **What an item is** — a node only; a node with the `sites` node split by its `site`
   label; or a general "item label" declared per family. The second: a site is a thing of
   its own by [0045](../decisions/0045-sites-are-a-node-their-hosts-report-for.md), and the
   general form has no second case yet.
2. **Which numbers a card shows** — compiled-in defaults per family; the same plus whatever
   needs attention; or series pinned in the interface. The second: a default list alone
   would hide a new metric and could hide a problem behind the numbers it chose. Pinning
   waits until the defaults prove wrong.
3. **A health score** — none; a count of `ok` series; a real 0–100 in the core. None: the
   formula still does not exist, and a count reads well exactly when one disk is critical.
4. **A forecast** — later, as a field of the engine with its own rule for when to stay
   quiet on noisy series.
5. **The tab** — between the timeline and the table; `/` keeps opening the timeline, so
   [0041](../decisions/0041-mission-control-folds-into-the-timeline.md) stands.

## Prototypes

Seven boards were drawn on synthetic data: tiles with a big number (Grafana, Datadog),
sections by kind (Uptime Kuma, Homepage), dense rows with sparklines (an APM host list),
rings (Netdata, Glances), a map of every series as a square (Datadog's host map), a briefing
that leads with what is wrong, and a bento grid with fleet-wide figures. Three item pages: a
grid of small charts beside the changes, one big chart with a list of series, and a report
with every chart on one time axis and the events drawn across them.

The reader chose the sections and the grid of small charts. Noted against the rest: the
bento's fleet-wide figures ("6 of 7 reporting", "least free space") are new fields of the
core, not a layout; the briefing's headline repeats the timeline's "Now"; the map shows
every series, which answers a different question from the card's few numbers; the rings
suit percentages only, so sites had none.

## The sections

Per node class was proposed, which splits servers from laptops. The reader wanted them
together, which gave a cleaner rule: by who collects the item — its own agent, the sites'
hosts, or the hub. It names no class but the two reserved ones, so a class the file adds
lands among the computers. Its known limit: finance and health will most likely be service
nodes and share a section with cloud storage until it splits.

## Decided while writing the spec

- **No norm band on the item's charts.** The prototype drew one, but the State API carries
  the norm and not the band, and the band of the current hour drawn across a 30-day window
  would mislead. An unusual series says its usual value instead, as the timeline's "Now"
  does.
- **Cards go worst first inside a section**, although the timeline keeps its lanes still: a
  section holds a handful of cards, and the sections themselves never move.

## What the spec reviews changed

- **"ok" means a level of `ok`**, not a stored threshold: a threshold unreadable or saved
  after the last tick holds no level, and the lanes call that "nothing watched". The node's
  silence subject is no series of an item, or every node would read "ok".
- **Attention's rows without its cap.** The list keeps five anomalies hub-wide, so a card
  could read "unusual" with no row saying why; the card takes attention's kinds and order,
  not its list.
- **A stale row is marked stale only**: a stored `critical` on a sensor switched off would
  have shown red on a green card.
- **Readable metric names were dropped.** The first draft named rows from the catalogue;
  every other page — the change lines on the same item page among them — names a series by
  its metric id, and names for some metrics only would cost code per metric against
  [0032](../decisions/0032-thresholds-are-set-in-the-interface.md).
- **A site is silent when its host is.** Otherwise a dead server's sites read "no fresh
  data" and sorted last, as a sleeping laptop does.
- **Each chart reads its own series**: one history answer holds at most 50 series and shares
  one window end, and a node may have more.
- **The page order was defined directly**: `/debug` puts unlabelled series in a table above
  its matrices, so "the order of its matrices" left load and memory unplaced.
